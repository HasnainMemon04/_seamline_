How we test, and what the numbers do not say

How we test

  • Rules first. Each protocol (P01, P02, P04) was frozen before the quantities it tests were computed, and lists what had already been seen. P04 was committed on its own on 5 Nov; its two logged changes came before any run.
  • Each test runs once. P04's code refuses to overwrite a result; each result records the code commit and the protocol's hash.
  • Negative results stay. The chosen model lost the held-out test to a simpler one (23.1% vs 17.5% error); a camera model lost to one multiplier; Aqua's spring dates failed in Punjab; Terra's fall was not seen in Sindh.
  • A separate checker. A second AI session, apart from the one that builds, checks plans and results. It recomputed every P02 number, checked P04's rules before any test quantity was computed, and on 9 Nov recomputed P04's four tests without seeing our numbers: every value matched.

What the numbers do not say

  • A detection is one fire pixel on one satellite pass: not a fire, not burned area, not emissions.
  • No detection is not "no fire": clouds and smoke hide burning. Unknown stays unknown, never zero.
  • S‑NPP is our reference scale, not ground truth.
  • No forecasts and no risk levels: only "unusual compared with this area's normal".
  • Pakistan only. 1.55 million archive detections, every file hash-checked. Built before the event with AI coding assistants; true dates in git.
