Seamline: putting MODIS and VIIRS fire detections on one scale for Pakistan

Methods and evidence report · NASA Space Apps 2026, challenge C08 · Muhammad Hasnain Memon (Karachi), with AI coding assistants · built 11 Nov 2026 19:56 UTC

Local research prototype. Pre-event work with true dates; not submitted. Agreement is measured against another satellite, not ground truth.

1. Question

Every autumn, during Punjab's smog season, people ask whether the burning seen this year is unusual, and whether it has grown over the decades. NASA FIRMS gives satellite fire detections back to 2000, but the instruments changed. MODIS (Terra 2000–, Aqua 2002–) has 1 km pixels; VIIRS (S-NPP 2012–, NOAA-20 2018–) has 375 m pixels and sees many small patches of burning that MODIS misses. Joining the two records makes burning appear to jump in 2012 because the observing system changed. Seamline puts the record on one declared scale and shows, for any province or rectangle in Pakistan, a seasonal calendar and how a month compares with that area's own normal.

Four newer questions were tested under Protocol P04 (section 7): does this October's press count for Pakistani Punjab reproduce, and how much of its rise since 2019 comes from added satellites? Can NOAA-20 carry the record after S-NPP's end on 2 November 2026? Does Terra's fall in Punjab's autumn share, seen under P02, also appear in Sindh and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa? And do the older and newer satellites agree on the season's middle and end dates (when half, and then 90%, of its detections were in)?

2. Quantity

H(region, month) = detected burning activity in S-NPP-VIIRS-equivalent detections: the number of quality-filtered S-NPP VIIRS 375 m fire pixels (Collection 2, standard processing; nominal or high confidence; FIRMS type 0) that S-NPP recorded, or is estimated to have recorded, in the region during the UTC month, day and night passes calibrated separately and summed. A detection is one fire pixel on one satellite pass: not a fire, not burned area, not emissions. No detection does not mean no fire: clouds, smoke and gaps between passes hide burning, and a hotspot-only record cannot see it.

Sources: Aqua MODIS converted to S-NPP-equivalent for 2003-01 to 2012-01; observed S-NPP from 2012-02 to 2025-12. S-NPP ended 2 Nov 2026, 15:40 UTC (NOAA NESDIS Update #4): its data delivery stopped then. NOAA-20 is the bridge: P01 tested it on 2024–25 (total error 4.4%), and it passed P04's frozen test on January–October 2026 (G03, section 7). By that test's rule, Punjab's record continues on converted NOAA-20 from November 2026, in whole months; other areas show NOAA-20 in its own units ("bridge tested in Punjab only"). The app's cached record still ends in December 2025, so 2026 months are not yet on its chart.

3. Data

52 NASA FIRMS files for Pakistan (MODIS MCD14ML 2000–2025, listed by the provider as C6.1, with file versions 6.2, 6.03 and 61.03; VIIRS C2 VNP14IMG 2012–2025 and VJ114IMG 2018–2025; 7-day NRT files of 2 Nov 2026), each with URL, SHA-256 and row count in data/manifest.json; province boundaries from geoBoundaries gbOpen PAK ADM1 (2019). A typed data contract checks platform, coordinates, time, file year, confidence (MODIS 0–100; VIIRS l/n/h), day/night flag, FRP and pixel size, version and type. Result: 1,553,471 archive rows, 0 rejected, 0 exact duplicates; 13,184 rows (0.85%) lie outside every province polygon along borders. In Punjab every satellite pass falls 05–10 UTC (day) or 16–22 UTC (night), so UTC-day months never split a pass. Months are labelled complete, partial, provider-missing (FIRMS missing-data table) or outside a satellite's record; missing is never shown as zero.

For P04's 2026 tests, 138 files from the FIRMS area API for Pakistan, in 5-day chunks (20 VIIRS_NOAA20_NRT, 46 VIIRS_NOAA20_SP, 6 VIIRS_NOAA21_NRT, 20 VIIRS_SNPP_NRT, 46 VIIRS_SNPP_SP), each hashed in data/manifest.json without the MAP_KEY, which is stored in no file. FIRMS's availability answer of 8 Nov set the stream: standard (SP) for Jan–Jul 2026 and near-real-time (NRT, no type field) for Aug–Oct, never mixed within a month. A refreshed missing-days list (178 entries, 8 Nov) was saved beside the committed one; P01, P02, G01 and G02 keep using the committed list.

4. Method and test plan (frozen before results)

Protocol P01 (experiments/PROTOCOL_P01.md) fixed the quantity, filters, regions, splits, candidate models, metrics and the selection rule before any Pakistan series was computed; six gap-filling clarifications were logged before the first experiment ran. That order is recorded in the session transcript, not in git: the code and the log were committed only after the experiments (docs/VALIDATION.md explains what can be verified). Candidates, each fitted for day and night passes separately on monthly region totals: M0 raw Aqua (what concatenation gives); M1 one ratio; M2 one ratio per calendar month shrunk towards M1 with 30 pseudo-detections; M3 Poisson log-linear with season terms; M4 M3 plus Terra. Selection: leave-one-year-out over 2014–2017 (Punjab), primary metric WAPE (total absolute error ÷ total detections); a model replaces M1 only if it beats it. Test: 2012-02 to 2013-12, run once after the selection was written to disk. Diagnostics: 2018–2025 (MODIS version changes, orbit drift) and Sindh (geographic transfer). Months with provider-reported missing days for any compared satellite are excluded from every score.

Later protocols follow the same pattern: P02 (camera model, clock test and burned area; section 6) and P04 (section 7). From P04 on, each protocol is committed on its own before any of its data is opened, every change is logged before the result it affects, and each result file records the code commit and the protocol's SHA-256.

6. Protocol P02 (3 Nov 2026): camera model, clock test and burned area

In brief, from docs/P02_RESULTS.md; the separate checking session recomputed every P02 number (docs/REVIEW_P02_2026-11-03.md). Camera model: a model in which the chance of detection rises with fire power did not beat one multiplier on held-out years and provinces; the negative result is kept. Clock test (Punjab): relative to what S-NPP's detections predict (P02's Q), Terra's October–November detections fell 79% from 2018–21 to 2023–25 (whole years as the unit, p = 0.029) as its pass moved earlier, from 10:49–10:58 to 9:40 local solar time; Aqua's, moving later, showed no clear change (+4%, p = 0.77). The test cannot tell whether burning moved, and no cause is claimed. Burned area: MODIS burned area rose 3.31× (2012–21 against 2003–11). The reading fixed in advance: "matches neither range; reported as it is". It is not independent of MODIS.

What P04 does not show

  • G01 claims no cause. A fall in Sindh or Khyber Pakhtunkhwa would only have made a satellite-side cause more likely; "not seen" in Sindh does not rule out the clock.
  • G02's dates describe past seasons only; they are not forecasts. Its tolerances equal the extremes of Punjab's earlier probe, so they have no headroom.
  • G03: a pass shows that converted NOAA-20 tracks S-NPP within the marks, not that the conversion beats no conversion. Only Punjab was scored.
  • G04 reproduces a press count; it does not make a sum of satellites comparable across years.

8. The application

A local web app (python -m c08 serve, no internet needed) lets a user pick a province on a map or draw a rectangle, choose years and a month, and see: the change-survives card; the record over time (naive, Aqua only, harmonised with interval); a year-by-month calendar marking months above or below the median of the same month in other years (factor 1.5); the selected month against normal for all three series, plus the naive stitch without NOAA-20; the latest complete days of near-real-time data against the same dates in earlier years; the area's own calibration quality; and every limitation. Exports: monthly values (CSV), the underlying detections (CSV, inside a ZIP when a selection has more than 300,000 rows), method and provenance (JSON), chart (SVG). Areas whose Aqua night detections are too few switch to day passes only. Display rules chosen after seeing results: areas with cross-validated WAPE above 50% are flagged as weakly supported, and an area is warned when its range spans more than 3 times or when M1 beat the selected model in its cross-validation.

9. Limitations

  • S-NPP is a reference convention, not truth. No ground-truth, burned-area or field validation was done.
  • Exposure (clear-sky observation) is unknown; zero detections is not zero fire.
  • The rough ranges (nominal 80%) are too narrow (59% test coverage) and ignore which calibration years were used; the ratio drifts over time and by season.
  • The held-out test (2012–13) sits next to the calibration years, so nothing tests the 2003–11 levels directly; the agreement of all four models supports the size of the change, not the modelled counts.
  • Small or mountainous areas have weak support; Balochistan's October–November range excludes its own point estimate, so the app leads with the range there.
  • The app's record ends in December 2025. The NOAA-20 bridge passed its frozen test on 2026 (G03), but 2026 months are not yet on the record chart; by the test's rule they are added in whole months, with converted NOAA-20 from November 2026 in Punjab only.
  • P04's results (8 Nov) were run before the checker's report on the change log and have not yet been reviewed by the checker. Punjab's G01 and G02 numbers are development numbers, seen before the test.
  • G03 mixes streams by month: standard January–July 2026, near-real-time with no type screen August–October; May had no matched days.
  • Bowtie overlap within a pass is not measured or removed, and a few S-NPP outage days are missing from the provider's list (for example 31 October 2019), which leaves those months slightly low.
  • Near-real-time files differ in processing from the archive and lack the type field; the latest-days panel compares like with like but does not correct this.
  • No forecast, risk, smoke attribution, crop type or emissions claims.

P04's tests are never re-run: each result file (experiments/P04_G01_terra_step, P04_G02_season_dates, P04_G03_bridge_2026, P04_G04_headline) records its code commit, the protocol's SHA-256 and the hash of every input, and python -m c08.p04 punjab-check repeats Punjab's development numbers without writing anything.
